# Staying Socially Connected as You Age

Social connection is one of the most important factors for healthy ageing. Research consistently shows that older adults who maintain strong social ties live longer, stay mentally sharper, and are less likely to develop depression, frailty, and dementia. In Singapore, a wide range of community programmes and resources are available to help you stay connected and active.

## Why Social Connection Matters

Humans are social beings at every age. Strong social relationships provide:

- **Better mental health:** Regular social interaction reduces the risk of depression and anxiety. Conversations, shared laughter, and emotional support all contribute to a positive mood.
- **Sharper mind:** Engaging with others stimulates your brain. Studies show that socially active older adults have a lower risk of cognitive decline and dementia.
- **Better physical health:** Socially connected people tend to be more physically active, eat better, and take better care of their health. Social isolation, on the other hand, has been linked to increased inflammation, higher blood pressure, and weakened immune function.
- **Greater sense of purpose:** Feeling needed and valued by others gives life meaning. Whether through volunteering, mentoring, or simply being a good friend, contributing to others' lives is deeply fulfilling.
- **Longer life:** Research shows that strong social connections are associated with a 50 percent greater likelihood of survival compared to those who are isolated. The health impact of loneliness is comparable to smoking 15 cigarettes a day.

## The Challenge of Social Isolation

Many older adults gradually become more isolated due to:
- Retirement and loss of workplace social networks
- Health problems that limit mobility
- Loss of a spouse or close friends
- Children and grandchildren who are busy with their own lives
- Moving to a new neighbourhood or downsizing
- Hearing or vision loss that makes communication difficult
- Fear of going out or being a burden

If you recognise any of these in yourself or a loved one, know that help and opportunities are available.

## Senior Activity Centres (SACs)

Singapore has over 100 Senior Activity Centres located in HDB estates across the island. These centres are run by various social service agencies and supported by the Agency for Integrated Care (AIC).

**What SACs offer:**
- Group exercise sessions, including Tai Chi, gentle yoga, and functional fitness
- Social activities like sing-along sessions, art classes, and game sessions
- Hot meals (some centres provide lunch or breakfast)
- Health screening and monitoring
- Befriending services for those who are more isolated
- Educational talks on health, nutrition, and other topics
- Outings and excursions

**How to find your nearest SAC:**
- Call the AIC Silver Line at 1800-650-6060
- Visit the AIC website and search for services near your postal code
- Ask at your nearest community club (CC) or residents' committee (RC)

## Community Clubs and Residents' Committees

Every HDB estate has a community club (CC) and residents' committees (RCs) that organise activities for residents of all ages, including many programmes designed for seniors.

**Activities commonly offered:**
- Exercise classes (Tai Chi, line dancing, aerobics)
- Language and culture classes
- Cooking classes
- Computer and smartphone skills
- Karaoke and music sessions
- Festival celebrations
- Day trips and excursions

Many of these activities are free or available at very low cost. Visit your CC or check the People's Association (PA) website for upcoming programmes.

## Volunteering: Giving Back and Gaining Purpose

Volunteering is one of the most rewarding ways to stay socially connected. It provides structure to your week, introduces you to new people, and gives you a strong sense of purpose.

**Volunteering opportunities for older adults in Singapore:**

- **Radin Mas Senior Volunteer Programme and similar schemes:** Many constituencies have structured volunteer programmes for active seniors.
- **Hospital and nursing home visits:** Many organisations coordinate visits to lonely elderly residents in care facilities.
- **Befriending services:** Organisations like TOUCH Community Services, Lions Befrienders, and SAGE Counselling Centre train volunteers to visit isolated older adults.
- **Mentoring and tutoring:** Share your skills and experience with younger generations through school-based or community programmes.
- **Community food distribution:** Help pack and distribute food to needy families through organisations like The Food Bank Singapore or Willing Hearts.
- **Religious organisations:** Many mosques, temples, and churches organise volunteer activities.

To find opportunities, contact:
- National Volunteer and Philanthropy Centre (NVPC): www.giving.sg
- Silver Generation Office (SGO) under AIC
- Your community club or residents' committee

## Intergenerational Connections

Building relationships across generations is valuable for both older and younger people. Older adults offer wisdom, experience, and stability, while younger people bring energy, fresh perspectives, and technology skills.

**Ways to build intergenerational connections:**
- Spend regular time with grandchildren or young relatives. Share stories, teach traditional skills, or learn new technology from them.
- Participate in intergenerational programmes at community clubs where seniors and youth do activities together.
- Offer to help neighbours with young children. Even a friendly conversation in the corridor builds connection.
- Join a multi-generational interest group, such as a gardening club or a walking group.

## Using Technology to Stay Connected

Technology can help bridge the distance between you and your loved ones, especially if mobility is limited.

**Simple ways to use technology for connection:**
- **Video calls:** Apps like WhatsApp Video, Zoom, or FaceTime let you see and talk to family and friends anywhere in the world. Ask a family member to help you set this up.
- **WhatsApp groups:** Family chat groups are a great way to share daily updates, photos, and messages.
- **Social media:** Facebook can help you stay in touch with old friends and classmates.
- **Online interest groups:** Join groups related to your hobbies or interests.

If you are not comfortable with technology, many senior activity centres and community clubs offer free digital literacy classes specifically for older adults. The Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) Seniors Go Digital programme provides one-on-one coaching at community locations across Singapore.

## Overcoming Barriers to Social Engagement

**"I feel shy or awkward going alone."**
Many people feel this way. Senior activity centres are welcoming places where staff will help you settle in. Going with a friend or neighbour can make the first visit easier. Remember that everyone there was new once.

**"I have health problems that make it hard to go out."**
Some SACs offer befriending visits to your home. Community clubs sometimes have transport arrangements for seniors. Even a phone call or video call from home counts as social connection.

**"I do not want to be a burden."**
Your presence enriches other people's lives just as theirs enriches yours. Social connection is a mutual exchange, not a one-way street.

**"My hearing or vision makes it hard."**
Let people know about your hearing or vision difficulties. Many will gladly speak louder, sit closer, or make other adjustments. Get your hearing and vision checked regularly, as untreated problems create unnecessary barriers.

**"I prefer to be alone."**
There is nothing wrong with enjoying solitude. But if you find that you are spending most days without any social interaction, consider adding even one regular social activity per week. The benefits are significant.

## Building Your Social Plan

Start small and build gradually:

1. **Identify one person** you can call or visit this week.
2. **Find one activity** at your nearest SAC or CC that interests you.
3. **Set a regular commitment**, such as attending a class every Tuesday or calling a friend every Sunday.
4. **Be open to new connections.** Not every interaction needs to be deep. A friendly chat with a neighbour or stall holder counts.
5. **Mix it up.** Combine in-person activities, phone calls, and digital communication.

Social connection is like exercise: the more you do it, the easier and more enjoyable it becomes. Invest in your relationships, and they will pay dividends in health, happiness, and quality of life.
