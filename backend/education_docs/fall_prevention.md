# Preventing Falls: A Practical Guide for Seniors

Falls are the most common cause of injury among older adults in Singapore. According to the Ministry of Health (MOH), about one in three adults aged 65 and above will experience a fall each year. The good news is that most falls can be prevented with simple steps. This guide will help you understand why falls happen and what you can do to stay safe.

## Why Do Falls Happen?

Falls usually result from a combination of factors rather than a single cause. Understanding these risk factors is the first step to prevention.

**Body-related factors:**
- Muscle weakness, especially in the legs
- Poor balance or unsteady gait
- Vision problems such as cataracts or glaucoma
- Dizziness when standing up (postural hypotension)
- Joint stiffness or pain from arthritis
- Foot problems like bunions or numbness
- Side effects from medications, especially sleeping pills, blood pressure tablets, or taking four or more medicines

**Environment-related factors:**
- Wet or slippery floors, especially in the bathroom and kitchen
- Poor lighting in hallways, stairs, and bedrooms
- Loose rugs, mats, or cluttered walkways
- Uneven floor surfaces or high door thresholds
- Lack of grab bars in the toilet and shower area
- Wearing slippers or shoes with poor grip

## Making Your Home Safer

Your home is where most falls happen. Here are practical changes you can make, many of which are supported by Singapore's Enhancement for Active Seniors (EASE) programme through HDB.

**Bathroom and Toilet:**
- Install grab bars next to the toilet bowl and inside the shower area. The HDB EASE programme provides subsidised installation of grab bars for eligible households.
- Use a non-slip bath mat or anti-slip stickers on the shower floor.
- Consider using a shower chair or stool so you can sit while bathing.
- Keep the floor dry. Wipe up water immediately after use.
- Use a raised toilet seat if you have difficulty sitting down or standing up.

**Bedroom:**
- Keep a nightlight on so you can see clearly if you wake up at night.
- Place a lamp or light switch within reach of your bed.
- Make sure the path from your bed to the toilet is clear of obstacles.
- Avoid placing items on the floor near your bed.

**Living Areas and Kitchen:**
- Remove loose rugs or secure them with double-sided tape.
- Keep electrical cords along walls and out of walkways.
- Arrange furniture so there is a clear walking path.
- Store frequently used items at waist height so you do not need to reach up or bend down.
- Wipe up spills on the kitchen floor immediately.

**Corridors and Common Areas:**
- Ensure all areas are well lit. Replace dim bulbs with brighter ones.
- Install handrails along corridors if possible.
- Wear shoes with non-slip soles when walking along the HDB common corridor.
- Be extra careful on rainy days when floors may be wet.

## Staying Strong to Prevent Falls

Physical activity is one of the best ways to prevent falls. Regular exercise strengthens your muscles, improves your balance, and keeps your joints flexible.

**Recommended activities:**
- Tai Chi has been shown to reduce falls by up to 50%. Many community clubs (CCs) and senior activity centres offer free or low-cost Tai Chi classes.
- Simple standing exercises like heel raises, toe raises, and single-leg stands can be done at home.
- Walking regularly helps maintain leg strength and cardiovascular fitness.
- Chair-based exercises are a good option if you have difficulty standing for long periods.

Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week, spread across several days. Even 10 minutes at a time counts.

## Regular Health Check-Ups

Visit your polyclinic or family doctor regularly to manage conditions that increase fall risk.

- **Eye checks:** Get your vision tested at least once a year. Update your glasses prescription if needed.
- **Medication review:** Ask your doctor or pharmacist to review all your medicines. Some medicines cause dizziness or drowsiness.
- **Blood pressure:** Have your blood pressure checked while sitting and standing. A drop when you stand (postural hypotension) can cause dizziness.
- **Foot care:** Check your feet regularly for sores, numbness, or nail problems. Wear well-fitting shoes with low heels and good grip.
- **Bone health:** Ask about bone density screening if you are at risk for osteoporosis. Strong bones break less easily if you do fall.

## What to Do If You Fall

Even with precautions, a fall can happen. Knowing what to do can prevent further injury.

**If you fall and cannot get up:**
1. Stay calm. Take a few slow breaths.
2. Check yourself for injuries. Do not try to get up quickly.
3. If you are hurt, call for help. Use your personal alert button if you have one, or call 995 for an ambulance.
4. If you are not hurt, roll onto your side, then push yourself up to a kneeling position. Use a sturdy chair or piece of furniture to help yourself stand.

**If you fall and can get up:**
1. Get up slowly using the method above.
2. Sit down and rest for a few minutes.
3. Check for any pain, bruising, or swelling.
4. Tell your family and your doctor about the fall, even if you feel fine. Some injuries like head bumps or hairline fractures may not show symptoms right away.

**After any fall:**
- Record when and where the fall happened, what you were doing, and how you felt before and after.
- Share this information with your doctor at your next visit.
- Consider whether any home modifications or exercise changes could help prevent future falls.

## Where to Get Help in Singapore

- **HDB EASE Programme:** Subsidised home modifications including grab bars and ramps. Apply through your Town Council.
- **Agency for Integrated Care (AIC):** Call the Silver Line at 1800-650-6060 for advice on community services.
- **Senior Activity Centres (SACs):** Offer exercise classes, health talks, and social activities in your neighbourhood.
- **Personal Alert Button:** The Senior Mobility and Enabling Fund (SMF) can help subsidise personal alert devices.
- **Emergency:** Call 995 for ambulance services.

Taking small steps today can make a big difference in keeping you safe and independent. Start with one or two changes and build from there.
