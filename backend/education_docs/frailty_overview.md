# Understanding Frailty: What It Is and What You Can Do

Frailty is a common condition in older adults where the body becomes more vulnerable to stress, illness, and injury. It is not a normal part of ageing, and in many cases, it can be slowed, stopped, or even reversed. This guide explains what frailty means, how it is assessed, and what practical steps you can take.

## What Is Frailty?

Frailty is a state where your body's reserves of strength, energy, and ability to recover are reduced. A frail person is more likely to:
- Fall and get injured
- Take longer to recover from illness or surgery
- Need help with daily activities like bathing, dressing, or cooking
- Be hospitalised

Frailty is different from disability or chronic disease, although they often overlap. You can have a chronic condition like diabetes and not be frail. Equally, you can be frail without having any specific disease.

Think of frailty as the body having less "buffer" to cope with challenges. A healthy older adult might recover quickly from a urinary infection, but a frail person could end up in hospital for weeks from the same infection.

## How Common Is Frailty?

In Singapore, studies suggest that about 5 to 6 percent of community-dwelling adults aged 65 and above are frail, while another 40 to 50 percent are pre-frail. These numbers increase significantly with age. By age 85 and above, frailty rates climb substantially.

Singapore's rapidly ageing population makes frailty prevention and management a national priority. The Ministry of Health's Action Plan for Successful Ageing and the Agency for Integrated Care's programmes specifically address frailty as part of healthy ageing.

## The Stages of Frailty

Frailty exists on a spectrum. Understanding where you are helps guide the right actions.

**Robust (Not Frail):**
You are strong, active, and can handle physical and mental stress well. You have no significant limitations in daily activities. This is the time to invest in prevention by staying physically active, eating well, and maintaining social connections.

**Pre-Frail:**
You are starting to show some signs of decline, such as reduced grip strength, slower walking speed, or lower energy levels. You can still do most daily activities independently. This is the most important stage for intervention because pre-frailty is highly reversible with the right actions.

**Frail:**
You have significant reductions in strength, energy, or physical function. You may need some help with daily activities. Falls are a greater risk. With consistent effort in exercise, nutrition, and medical management, some improvement is still possible.

**Severely Frail:**
You are dependent on others for most daily activities. Recovery from illness takes a long time. The focus at this stage is on maintaining current function, preventing further decline, and ensuring comfort and quality of life.

## How Is Frailty Assessed?

Several tools are used to assess frailty. SilverGait uses a combination of three validated assessment tools:

**1. Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS):**
A scale from 1 (very fit) to 9 (terminally ill) based on your overall activity level, symptoms, and dependence on others. It looks at how well you manage daily life.

**2. Katz Index of Independence:**
Measures your ability to perform 6 basic daily activities: bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring (moving from bed to chair), continence (bladder and bowel control), and feeding. Each activity you can do independently scores 1 point, with 6 being fully independent.

**3. Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB):**
A practical physical test with three components:
- **Standing balance:** Can you stand with feet together, in semi-tandem position, and in full tandem position?
- **Gait speed:** How fast can you walk 4 metres at your normal pace?
- **Chair stand:** Can you stand up from a chair 5 times without using your arms?

The SPPB scores from 0 to 12 points. A score of 10 to 12 is good, 7 to 9 suggests moderate limitation, and below 7 indicates significant limitation.

## Can Frailty Be Reversed?

Yes, especially in the early stages. Research consistently shows that pre-frailty can be reversed in many cases, and even frail individuals can improve their function.

The key interventions are:

### 1. Physical Exercise
This is the single most effective intervention for frailty. A combination of:
- **Resistance training** (strengthening exercises) 2 to 3 times per week
- **Balance training** 3 times per week
- **Walking** at least 150 minutes per week

Even 10 minutes of exercise at a time provides benefits. The Otago Exercise Programme is specifically designed for older adults and has strong evidence for reducing falls and improving strength.

### 2. Nutrition
Adequate protein intake is critical for maintaining and rebuilding muscle:
- Aim for 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight daily
- Spread protein intake across all meals
- Stay well hydrated with 6 to 8 glasses of fluid daily
- Consider vitamin D supplementation if deficient

### 3. Medical Management
- Regular medication reviews to reduce harmful polypharmacy
- Management of chronic conditions like diabetes, hypertension, and arthritis
- Screening and treatment of depression, which worsens frailty
- Regular vision and hearing checks

### 4. Social Engagement
Social isolation is a significant contributor to frailty. Staying connected through:
- Regular contact with family and friends
- Participation in community activities
- Volunteering or mentoring
- Attending senior activity centres

## What You Can Do at Each Stage

**If you are Robust:**
- Stay active with regular exercise including strength and balance training
- Eat a balanced diet with adequate protein
- Stay socially connected
- Get regular health screenings
- Continue to challenge yourself physically and mentally

**If you are Pre-Frail:**
- Start a structured exercise programme (Otago or similar) at least 3 times per week
- Increase your protein intake, especially at breakfast and lunch
- Address any unsteadiness or fear of falling early
- Talk to your doctor about any new tiredness, weakness, or weight loss
- Join group exercise classes for motivation and social connection

**If you are Frail:**
- Work with a physiotherapist to create a safe, personalised exercise plan
- Focus on chair-based and supported exercises if standing is difficult
- Ensure adequate nutrition; consider oral nutritional supplements if appetite is poor
- Review all medications with your doctor or pharmacist
- Accept appropriate help while maintaining as much independence as possible
- Use walking aids if recommended

**If you are Severely Frail:**
- Gentle, assisted exercises to maintain current function
- Focus on good nutrition and comfort
- Caregiver support and respite care are important
- Advance care planning discussions with family and healthcare team
- Make use of community support services
