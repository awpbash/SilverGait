# Managing Chronic Conditions in Later Life

Many older adults in Singapore live with one or more chronic conditions. Diabetes, hypertension (high blood pressure), and arthritis are among the most common. While these conditions cannot be cured, they can be well managed so that you can live a full and active life. Good management of chronic disease also helps prevent frailty and maintain independence. This guide covers the basics of managing these common conditions.

## Diabetes in Older Adults

Diabetes is very common in Singapore, affecting about 1 in 3 adults aged 60 and above. It occurs when your body cannot properly regulate blood sugar levels. Uncontrolled diabetes can lead to serious complications including heart disease, kidney damage, vision loss, nerve damage, and slow wound healing.

### Managing Your Blood Sugar

**Regular monitoring:**
- Check your blood sugar as recommended by your doctor. Some people need to check daily, others less frequently.
- Keep a record of your readings and bring it to every doctor visit.
- Know your target range. Your doctor will tell you what your blood sugar should be before meals and after meals.
- Get your HbA1c tested every 3 to 6 months. This test shows your average blood sugar over the past 2 to 3 months. For most older adults, an HbA1c below 7 to 8 percent is a reasonable target, but your doctor will set the right goal for you.

**Diet:**
- Choose wholegrain options: brown rice, wholemeal bread, and oats instead of white rice and white bread.
- Eat regular, balanced meals. Do not skip meals, as this can cause blood sugar swings.
- Limit sweet drinks, including kopi and teh with sugar. Ask for "siu dai" (less sweet) or "kosong" (no sugar) versions.
- Fill half your plate with vegetables at each meal.
- Watch portion sizes, especially for rice, noodles, and bread.
- Choose fresh fruit over fruit juice. Eat fruits in moderation (2 servings per day).

**Physical activity:**
- Regular exercise helps your body use insulin better and lowers blood sugar.
- Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week (such as brisk walking).
- Check your feet before and after exercise for any sores or blisters, especially if you have nerve damage.

**Foot care:**
- Diabetes can cause nerve damage and poor circulation in the feet, making you more vulnerable to infections and slow-healing wounds.
- Inspect your feet daily for cuts, blisters, redness, or swelling.
- Wash your feet daily with warm (not hot) water and dry thoroughly, especially between the toes.
- Wear well-fitting, closed shoes. Avoid walking barefoot.
- See a podiatrist if you have foot problems. Many polyclinics offer diabetic foot screening.

### Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycaemia)

If you take diabetes medications, especially insulin or sulfonylureas, you need to know the signs of low blood sugar:
- Sweating, shaking, or trembling
- Feeling dizzy, weak, or confused
- Sudden hunger
- Fast heartbeat

**What to do:** Immediately eat or drink something sweet, such as 3 glucose tablets, half a glass of sweet drink, or a tablespoon of honey. Then eat a small snack with carbohydrate and protein. If symptoms persist or worsen, seek medical help immediately.

## High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

Hypertension is often called the "silent killer" because it usually has no symptoms. It significantly increases the risk of stroke, heart attack, kidney disease, and dementia. About half of Singaporeans aged 60 to 69 have hypertension.

### Managing Your Blood Pressure

**Regular monitoring:**
- Check your blood pressure regularly, either at the polyclinic, a pharmacy, or at home with a home blood pressure monitor.
- Your target is usually below 140/90 mmHg, but your doctor may set a different target based on your health.
- Take readings at the same time each day, sitting down and resting for 5 minutes first.
- Record your readings and bring them to your doctor.

**Lifestyle changes:**
- **Reduce salt.** Aim for less than 5 grams of salt per day (about 1 teaspoon). Be mindful of hidden salt in soy sauce, oyster sauce, fish sauce, preserved foods, and processed snacks. Use herbs, garlic, ginger, and lime juice for flavour instead.
- **Maintain a healthy weight.** Even losing 2 to 3 kilograms can lower blood pressure.
- **Exercise regularly.** 150 minutes of moderate activity per week helps lower blood pressure.
- **Limit alcohol.** No more than 1 standard drink per day.
- **Manage stress.** Practice relaxation techniques like deep breathing or Tai Chi.
- **Stop smoking.** Smoking damages blood vessels and raises blood pressure.

**Medication:**
- Take your blood pressure medication as prescribed, even if you feel fine. Hypertension has no symptoms, so you cannot tell by how you feel whether your pressure is controlled.
- Do not stop or change your medication without talking to your doctor.
- Some blood pressure medicines can cause dizziness when you stand up. Stand up slowly and hold onto something stable.

## Arthritis

Arthritis causes joint pain, stiffness, and swelling. Osteoarthritis, the most common type in older adults, results from wear and tear of the joint cartilage. It commonly affects the knees, hips, hands, and spine.

### Managing Arthritis

**Staying active:**
- This may seem counterintuitive when joints hurt, but regular gentle exercise is one of the best treatments for arthritis.
- Movement keeps joints flexible, strengthens the muscles that support them, and reduces stiffness.
- Good activities: walking, swimming, water aerobics, Tai Chi, gentle cycling, and chair exercises.
- Avoid high-impact activities like running or jumping if they worsen your pain.
- Start gently and gradually increase duration and intensity.

**Weight management:**
- Extra weight puts additional stress on your joints, especially the knees. Each kilogram of extra weight puts about 4 kilograms of extra pressure on your knee joints.
- Even modest weight loss can significantly reduce pain and improve function.

**Pain management:**
- Apply heat (warm towel or heat pack) to stiff joints before exercise, and cold packs after activity if joints are swollen.
- Paracetamol is often the first choice for pain relief. Take it regularly as directed, not just when pain is severe.
- Anti-inflammatory medicines (like ibuprofen) can help but should be used with caution in older adults due to stomach and kidney risks. Use them only as directed by your doctor.
- Topical pain relief creams or patches can help for localised joint pain.
- Your doctor may recommend physiotherapy, joint injections, or in severe cases, surgery.

**Joint protection:**
- Use assistive devices to reduce strain on painful joints: jar openers, long-handled reachers, ergonomic kitchen tools.
- Take rest breaks during activities that stress your joints.
- Use a walking stick on the opposite side of your painful knee or hip to reduce load.
- Wear supportive, cushioned shoes.

## General Tips for Managing Chronic Conditions

### Take an Active Role

- Understand your conditions. Ask your doctor to explain in simple terms what each condition is and why each medication is prescribed.
- Set realistic goals with your healthcare team. For example, "I want to walk to the market and back without feeling breathless."
- Track your health numbers (blood sugar, blood pressure, weight) and notice trends.

### Regular Follow-Up

- Keep all your clinic appointments, even when you feel well.
- Chronic conditions need ongoing monitoring and adjustment of treatment.
- Prepare for appointments: write down questions, bring your medication list, and bring your monitoring records.

### Polypharmacy Awareness

- If you see multiple doctors (polyclinic, specialist, TCM), make sure each knows all the medicines you take.
- Ask for a medication review at least once a year.
- Report any new symptoms that might be medication side effects.

### Healthy Habits That Help All Chronic Conditions

- Eat a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruits, wholegrains, and lean protein.
- Stay physically active within your abilities.
- Maintain a healthy weight.
- Get enough sleep (7 to 8 hours).
- Manage stress through relaxation, social connection, and meaningful activities.
- Avoid smoking and limit alcohol.

## Singapore Resources

- **War on Diabetes programme (HPB):** Free resources, screenings, and programmes nationwide.
- **CHAS (Community Health Assist Scheme):** Subsidised treatment at participating GP clinics for eligible Singaporeans.
- **Chronic Disease Management Programme (CDMP):** Allows use of MediSave for outpatient treatment of diabetes, hypertension, and other chronic conditions.
- **Polyclinics:** Your primary healthcare provider for chronic disease management.
- **National Kidney Foundation:** Screening and support for kidney health.
- **Singapore Heart Foundation:** Education and resources for heart health.

Managing chronic conditions well is a cornerstone of healthy ageing. With the right knowledge, support, and habits, you can live well with these conditions and maintain your independence for years to come.
