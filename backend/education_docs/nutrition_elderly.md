# Nutrition for Healthy Ageing

Good nutrition plays a vital role in maintaining strength, energy, and independence as you age. Older adults in Singapore face unique nutritional challenges, including reduced appetite, difficulty chewing, and the need for more protein to prevent muscle loss. This guide provides practical advice based on recommendations from the Health Promotion Board (HPB) and the Agency for Integrated Care (AIC).

## Why Nutrition Matters More as You Age

As we get older, our body changes in ways that affect nutrition:
- **Muscle loss (sarcopenia):** After age 50, we lose about 1 to 2 percent of muscle mass each year. Without enough protein and exercise, this leads to weakness and frailty.
- **Reduced appetite:** Many older adults eat less due to changes in taste, smell, or feeling full more quickly.
- **Slower digestion:** Food moves more slowly through the gut, which can cause constipation and bloating.
- **Bone thinning:** Lower calcium and vitamin D levels increase the risk of osteoporosis and fractures.
- **Medication effects:** Some medicines reduce appetite or interfere with how your body absorbs nutrients.

Eating well is one of the most effective ways to slow down frailty, maintain your energy levels, and support your immune system.

## Protein: The Most Important Nutrient for Older Adults

Protein is essential for maintaining and rebuilding muscle. Older adults need more protein than younger people because the body becomes less efficient at using protein as we age.

**How much protein do you need?**
The HPB recommends that older adults aim for 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day. For a 60 kg person, that is 60 to 72 grams of protein daily. If you are recovering from illness or very active, you may need even more.

**Good sources of protein (with local options):**

| Food | Protein (approx.) |
|---|---|
| 1 palm-sized piece of chicken or fish | 20-25g |
| 1 egg | 6-7g |
| 1 cup of milk or soy milk (fortified) | 7-8g |
| 1 block of firm tofu (about 150g) | 12-15g |
| 1 cup of cooked dhal or lentils | 12-18g |
| 1 small tin of sardines | 15-20g |
| 2 tablespoons of peanut butter | 7g |

**Tips to eat more protein:**
- Include a protein-rich food at every meal and snack.
- Add a hard-boiled egg or a glass of milk to your breakfast.
- Choose dishes like fish porridge, chicken rice (ask for extra chicken), or mee soto with extra chicken.
- Snack on roasted nuts, cheese, or yoghurt between meals.
- If chewing is difficult, choose softer options like steamed fish, tofu, scrambled eggs, or dhal.

## Staying Hydrated

Dehydration is common among older adults because the sense of thirst decreases with age. Dehydration can cause confusion, dizziness, constipation, and urinary tract infections.

**How much fluid do you need?**
Aim for 6 to 8 glasses (about 1.5 to 2 litres) of fluid per day. In Singapore's hot and humid climate, you may need more, especially if you are active or spending time outdoors.

**Tips to stay hydrated:**
- Keep a water bottle nearby and sip regularly throughout the day.
- Drink a glass of water with each meal and snack.
- Soups, porridge, and watery fruits like watermelon and oranges count towards your fluid intake.
- Limit caffeinated drinks (coffee, tea) to 2 to 3 cups a day, as caffeine can increase fluid loss.
- If you dislike plain water, add a squeeze of lemon or try barley water or chrysanthemum tea (with less sugar).

## Calcium and Vitamin D for Strong Bones

Calcium and vitamin D work together to keep your bones strong and reduce the risk of fractures.

**Calcium:** Aim for 1,000 mg per day. Good sources include milk, yoghurt, cheese, tofu (made with calcium sulfate), ikan bilis (dried anchovies), and dark leafy vegetables like kailan and chye sim.

**Vitamin D:** Most Singaporeans do not get enough vitamin D, even with our sunny weather, because many older adults stay indoors. Ask your doctor about vitamin D supplements. A daily dose of 800 to 1,000 IU is commonly recommended for older adults.

**Tips:**
- Drink 2 glasses of milk or fortified soy milk daily.
- Eat ikan bilis as a snack or add them to porridge and rice.
- Try to get 10 to 15 minutes of morning sunlight (before 10am) on your arms and legs several times a week.

## Fibre for Digestive Health

Constipation is a common problem for older adults. Eating enough fibre and drinking plenty of fluids can help.

**Good sources of fibre:**
- Brown rice, wholemeal bread, and oats
- Fruits like papaya, banana, and guava
- Vegetables like long beans, broccoli, and spinach
- Beans and lentils

Aim for at least 2 servings of fruit and 2 servings of vegetables each day. If you are not used to eating much fibre, increase your intake gradually to avoid bloating.

## Vitamins and Supplements

A balanced diet should provide most of the nutrients you need. However, some older adults may benefit from supplements:

- **Vitamin D:** As mentioned, many Singaporeans are deficient. Your doctor can check your levels with a blood test.
- **Vitamin B12:** Older adults absorb less B12 from food. Symptoms of deficiency include fatigue, numbness, and memory problems. Ask your doctor to check if you are on long-term acid-reducing medication.
- **Iron:** Important if you eat very little meat. Signs of deficiency include tiredness and breathlessness.
- **Oral nutritional supplements (ONS):** Products like Ensure, Glucerna, or Sustagen can help if you are losing weight unintentionally or eating very little. Consult your doctor or dietitian before starting.

Do not take supplements without medical advice, as some can interact with your medications.

## Practical Meal Planning Tips

**If you have a poor appetite:**
- Eat smaller meals more frequently (5 to 6 small meals instead of 3 large ones).
- Choose nutrient-dense foods rather than filling up on plain rice or bread.
- Eat your protein first when you are most hungry.
- Make meals a social activity. Eating with family or friends often improves appetite.

**If you have difficulty chewing or swallowing:**
- Choose soft foods like porridge, steamed fish, scrambled eggs, mashed potato, soft tofu, and ripe fruits.
- Cut food into small pieces or mince it.
- Add gravy or sauce to make food easier to swallow.
- If swallowing is a persistent problem, ask your doctor for a referral to a speech therapist.

**Budget-friendly eating:**
- Shop at wet markets in the late morning for discounted produce.
- Canned fish (sardines, tuna) is affordable and high in protein and omega-3 fats.
- Eggs are one of the most affordable and complete protein sources.
- Dried beans and lentils are cheap and nutritious.
- Many senior activity centres and community clubs offer subsidised meals.

## When to Seek Help

See your doctor or ask for a referral to a dietitian if:
- You have lost weight without trying (more than 2 to 3 kg in a month)
- You feel very tired, weak, or unwell despite eating
- You have difficulty swallowing or frequent choking
- You have a chronic condition like diabetes, kidney disease, or heart failure that requires a special diet

In Singapore, you can access dietetic services through polyclinics, restructured hospitals, and some community health centres. The Pioneer Generation and Merdeka Generation packages provide additional healthcare subsidies.

Eating well does not need to be complicated or expensive. Small changes to your daily meals can make a big difference to your strength, energy, and quality of life.
