# Making Your Home Safe and Accessible

Most falls among older adults happen at home. Simple modifications to your living environment can significantly reduce the risk of falls and injuries, making it easier for you to move around safely and maintain your independence. This guide covers practical changes you can make in each area of your home, with specific reference to Singapore's HDB living environment and available support programmes.

## Assessing Your Home for Hazards

Take a walk through your home with fresh eyes, looking for things that could cause a trip, slip, or fall. Better yet, ask a family member or friend to help you, as they may spot hazards you have gotten used to.

**Key questions to ask:**
- Are there items on the floor that could trip me?
- Is the lighting bright enough to see clearly?
- Are the floors slippery, especially when wet?
- Can I reach things I need without stretching up high or bending low?
- Do I have something sturdy to hold onto where I need it most?
- Can I get to the toilet safely at night?

## Bathroom and Toilet Safety

The bathroom is the most dangerous room in the home for older adults. Wet, slippery surfaces combined with the need to move between sitting and standing create a high-risk environment.

**Essential modifications:**

**Grab Bars:**
- Install grab bars next to the toilet bowl to help you sit down and stand up safely. Place one on the wall beside the toilet and consider an angled bar for additional support.
- Install grab bars inside the shower area, including a vertical bar for steadying yourself and a horizontal bar for support while washing.
- Make sure grab bars are securely fixed into the wall structure, not just surface-mounted. They must support your full body weight.

**Non-Slip Surfaces:**
- Apply anti-slip stickers or strips to the shower floor and bathtub.
- Use a non-slip bath mat outside the shower area to step onto when you get out.
- Consider anti-slip treatment for bathroom floor tiles.

**Shower Seating:**
- Use a shower chair or wall-mounted fold-down seat so you can sit while bathing. This is especially important if you feel dizzy or tire easily.
- Choose a seat with non-slip rubber feet and drainage holes.

**Raised Toilet Seat:**
- If you have difficulty getting on and off the toilet, a raised toilet seat adds 5 to 10 centimetres of height, making it much easier. Some models come with built-in armrests.

**Other bathroom tips:**
- Keep the bathroom floor dry. Mop up water immediately after use.
- Use a hand-held showerhead so you can rinse yourself while seated.
- Ensure good lighting in the bathroom. A waterproof LED light above the mirror and near the shower area is helpful.
- Store toiletries at an easy-to-reach height. Avoid bending or stretching.

## Bedroom Safety

**Lighting:**
- Place a nightlight or motion-sensor light between your bedroom and the toilet so you can see clearly when you get up at night.
- Keep a lamp or torch within arm's reach of your bed.
- Make sure the light switch is accessible from the doorway and the bed.

**Bed height:**
- Your bed should be at a height where your feet touch the floor when you sit on the edge. This makes getting in and out easier. If your bed is too low, bed risers can help. If too high, consider a lower bed frame.

**Floor clearance:**
- Keep the floor around your bed completely clear of clutter, shoes, bags, and cables.
- Avoid loose rugs next to the bed.
- If you use a bedside table, make sure it is sturdy enough to lean on if needed.

**Assistive devices:**
- A bed rail or handle can help you turn over in bed and get up safely.
- Keep your phone or a personal alert button within reach while sleeping.

## Living Room and Common Areas

**Furniture arrangement:**
- Arrange furniture to create clear, wide walking paths. You should be able to walk from one room to another without having to squeeze between objects or step over anything.
- Remove or secure loose rugs with double-sided tape or non-slip backing. Better yet, remove them entirely.
- Choose chairs with sturdy armrests that make it easier to sit down and stand up.
- Avoid low chairs and sofas that are hard to get out of. Seat height should be about knee height.

**Electrical safety:**
- Keep electrical cords along walls and out of walking paths.
- Use cord covers or tape to secure any cords that cross walkways.
- Do not overload electrical outlets with too many plugs.

**Lighting:**
- Ensure all rooms and hallways are well lit. Replace dim bulbs with brighter LED bulbs.
- Use motion-sensor lights in hallways and the entrance area.
- Keep torches in accessible locations in case of power cuts.

## Kitchen Safety

**Storage:**
- Store frequently used items like cups, plates, and cooking essentials between waist and shoulder height. Avoid storing things on high shelves that require a step stool.
- If you must use high shelves, use a stable step stool with a handrail, never a chair.
- Keep heavy items like rice and oil at counter level.

**Floor:**
- Wipe up spills immediately. Oil and water on kitchen tiles are very slippery.
- Place a non-slip mat in front of the sink and stove.

**Cooking safety:**
- Use a timer to remind you of food on the stove.
- Consider an induction cooker if you sometimes forget to turn off the gas.
- Wear close-fitting clothing while cooking to avoid catching on burner flames.
- Keep a fire extinguisher or fire blanket accessible.

## Corridor and Entrance (HDB-specific)

**HDB common corridor:**
- Keep your entrance area clear of shoe racks, potted plants, and other items that narrow the walkway.
- Ensure your front door area is well lit. Replace the bulb promptly if it goes out.
- Be cautious on rainy days when the corridor floor is wet. Wear shoes with non-slip soles.

**Gate and door thresholds:**
- If the threshold between rooms or at the front door is a tripping hazard, consider adding a small ramp or removing the threshold if possible.
- Ensure doors can be opened easily. Replace stiff handles with lever-type handles.

## The HDB EASE Programme

The Enhancement for Active Seniors (EASE) programme is a government initiative by HDB that provides subsidised home improvement items for elderly residents. Eligible items include:

- **Grab bars** in the toilet and bathroom
- **Slip-resistant treatment** for floor tiles in the bathroom
- **Ramps** to replace raised thresholds at the main door and bathroom entrance
- **Lever-type door handles** to replace round knobs

**How to apply:**
- Contact your Town Council to enquire about EASE.
- Eligibility is based on age (65 and above) and household income.
- Subsidies can cover up to 95 percent of the cost depending on income level.
- Installation is done by approved contractors.

## Emergency Preparedness

**Personal Alert Systems:**
- Consider wearing a personal alert button (pendant or wristband) that you can press to call for help if you fall or feel unwell.
- The Senior Mobility and Enabling Fund (SMF) can subsidise the cost of personal alert devices.
- Some systems connect to a 24-hour monitoring centre, while others call your family members directly.

**Emergency contacts:**
- Keep a list of emergency contacts (family, doctor, ambulance 995) in a visible place, such as on the refrigerator.
- Save these numbers on speed dial in your phone.
- Ensure your neighbours know how to reach your family if needed.

**Other preparations:**
- Keep a torch and charged phone by your bed.
- Know where your main electrical switch and water valve are located.
- If you live alone, arrange for someone to check on you daily, either in person or by phone.

## Getting a Professional Assessment

If you are unsure what modifications you need, you can request a home safety assessment through:
- **Agency for Integrated Care (AIC):** Can arrange for an occupational therapist to assess your home.
- **Hospital discharge team:** If you are being discharged after a fall or illness, the hospital OT team may visit your home.
- **Polyclinic referral:** Your doctor can refer you for a home assessment.

Small changes to your home environment can make a big difference in keeping you safe and independent. Start with the most important changes, like bathroom grab bars and better lighting, and work from there.
